# Persona: experienced operator

## Known facts

- Has an existing profitable offer and customer list.
- Has a fulfillment bottleneck and wants leverage, not a new idea.

## Missing facts

- Bottleneck location, unit economics, service-level commitments, customer consent, current tools, team capacity, quality risks, and authority to change the workflow.

## Unsuitable routes

- Launching an unrelated offer, automating customer commitments without approval, reducing quality without measurement, or using customer data outside consent and contractual limits.

## Credible primary route

- Map one fulfillment bottleneck and test a human-reviewed standard operating procedure, template, or internal copilot that removes a bounded manual step while preserving quality controls.

## Credible fallback

- If automation access or data approval is unavailable, run a manual capacity experiment: assign a clear owner, use a standardized handoff, and measure turnaround and defect rate for one customer cohort.

## Smallest payable test

- Within 24 hours, select one bottleneck, baseline a small work sample, create a reviewable artifact, and have the operator approve its use for one controlled batch.

## Continue threshold

- Continue when the controlled batch shows a documented **Estimated economic value** with formula, assumptions, and sensitivity, while quality and service-level metrics stay within the operator's approved guardrails. Go only if the documented value exceeds fully loaded test cost and expected contribution margin remains positive.

## Failure conditions

- Do not expose customer data, change live delivery, or make external commitments without operator approval.
- Stop if quality falls, the savings model lacks assumptions, the bottleneck is not measurable, or a new idea would distract from the existing offer.
