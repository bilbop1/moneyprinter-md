# Source grades

Assign a grade to the evidence, not to the desired answer. Record each claim's
direct URL, source class, publication date when available, event date when
relevant, and exact review date. If one of URL, source class, or review date is
missing, label the claim unverified rather than assigning it a grade.

- **A — primary or official evidence**: first-party buyer statements with
  permission, official policy, current official pricing, a dated procurement
  request, or another source directly documenting the claim.
- **B — replicated or multi-source evidence**: independent, relevant sources
  that consistently support the claim but are not individually primary or
  official proof of it.
- **C — practitioner heuristic**: a clearly attributed rule of thumb, operator
  observation, or non-replicated secondary interpretation. It cannot establish
  demand or willingness to pay by itself.
- **H — project hypothesis**: an explicit proposed test assumption with no
  sufficient external support. It is not evidence and must never be presented
  as verified demand.

Use the weakest material grade for an opportunity row. Grades describe source
quality, not commercial certainty: show confidence separately and include
contrary evidence. Do not upgrade a source because it agrees with the project.
